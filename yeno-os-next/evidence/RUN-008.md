## The invariant this RUN exists to prove

> a boundary that came due is served **at most once**, and time the OS spent dead becomes one owed occurrence, never a burst

Two independent switches must both be on before anything fires — the master scheduler switch and the individual
schedule — and the emergency stop overrides both. Arming either one needs Owner provenance and a reason.

## What was built

1. `migrations/0013_scheduler.sql` — replaces the empty `schedules` placeholder from `0012` (only after proving it is
   empty, via a `CHECK (rows = 0)` scratch table, so a non-empty placeholder fails the migration closed) and adds:
   - `schedules` (unique name, queue, kind, payload, `interval_ms >= 1000`, `enabled`, `catchup`, authority,
     `next_due_at`, `last_fired_at`, `fire_count`, Owner reason and provenance),
   - `schedule_fires` with `UNIQUE (schedule_id, due_at)` — the at-most-once rule as a database constraint,
   - `schedule_soak_cycles` for per-cycle crash evidence,
   - triggers: arming needs the Owner and a reason, immutable schedule intent (name/kind/payload/queue/authority/
     interval/id), monotonic `next_due_at` and `fire_count`, no schedule deletion, immutable and undeletable firing
     history, and a trigger that refuses to let a soak cycle claiming a double fire or a missing kill be recorded as
     a pass.
2. `src/domain/scheduler.mjs` — `createSchedule` (born disarmed), `setScheduleEnabled` (Owner + reason),
   `dueSchedules`, `tickScheduler`, `nextBoundary`, `scheduleReport`, `schedulerAcceptance`.
   One tick does enqueue + fire row + boundary advance in a single `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction, dedupes the job on
   `sched:<name>@<due_at>`, and treats a `schedule_fires` uniqueness race as an occurrence another tick already served.
   Missed time collapses to the first strictly-future boundary; `catchup` mode hands out one owed occurrence per tick
   and never a burst.
3. `scripts/scheduler-tick-worker.mjs` — a real OS process that runs one tick and can `SIGKILL` itself either
   **in-transaction** (from a `beforeCommit` hook, after the rows are written but before commit) or **after-commit**,
   blocking afterwards so no further JavaScript runs.
4. `scripts/scheduler-soak.mjs` — process-level crash soak: real child process, alternating kill points, then a brand
   new process ticking again as if after a reboot. Either the crash rolled the occurrence back and the restart served
   it, or the crash committed it and the restart left it alone; both must end at exactly one fire and one job.
5. `scripts/scheduler-exercise.mjs` — one real pass on the kernel ledger: both switches off → tick refused
   `SCHEDULER_OFF`; switch on, schedule disarmed → nothing fires; Owner arms it → exactly one job; a replayed tick →
   nothing; six owed hours → one fire, not six. Ends by disarming the schedule and returning the scheduler switch and
   the emergency stop to their prior (safe) position.
6. `scripts/red-team-queue.mjs` — 13 new scheduler probes (42 total). The bypass-class taxonomy is applied schema
   (`0010`), so scheduler probes use the existing `RAW_SQL`/`GOVERNOR_API` classes and the suite asserts scheduler
   coverage by count: fewer than 13 refused scheduler probes fails the suite instead of silently shrinking it.
7. `scripts/independent-validation.mjs` — new `scheduler_recomputed` check, computed from raw `schedules` /
   `schedule_fires` / `schedule_soak_cycles` rows, never from a view: double-served boundaries, fires pointing at
   missing jobs, armed schedules without Owner provenance or a reason, sub-second intervals, fire counts below the
   recorded history, soak rows calling a double fire or a missing kill a pass, and a G4 marked `PASSED` without a
   clean soak covering both kill points.
8. `scripts/assert-seeded-defect-detected.mjs` — 3 new seeded defects (17 total): a boundary fired twice, a soak cycle
   that lies about it, and an armed schedule with no Owner.
9. `scripts/local-ci.mjs` — `scheduler_crash_soak` is now a CI step (16 steps).

## Why the soak is not vacuous

- The tick worker is a real OS process killed with `SIGKILL`, not a simulated failure, and the kill happens **inside the
  transaction** in half the cycles — after the job row, the fire row and the boundary advance are all written, before
  the commit. If any of the three were in its own transaction, the restart would double-fire or lose the occurrence.
- Every cycle then counts raw rows: `schedule_fires` for that exact `(schedule_id, due_at)` and `jobs` for that exact
  dedupe key. Anything other than `1` and `1` fails the cycle, and `0013` refuses to record a passing cycle that says
  otherwise.
- A soak-flag bug was found and fixed while writing this: `--no-record` was parsed as a key/value pair, so the flag
  never took effect and two exploratory 4-cycle runs were recorded in the kernel. They are real passing soaks and stay
  on the record (`schedule_soak_cycles` is append-only); the gate rests on `SSOAK-G4-001` only.
